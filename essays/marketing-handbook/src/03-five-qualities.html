Ch. I · How I work

The five qualities.

Every piece of work I respect carries at least two of these. The best carry four.

i.

Nostalgic

Borrows a feeling from the past to land an idea in the present. Memory is the cheapest emotional bandwidth.

A childhood meal in a fintech campaign. Old typewriter sounds in a productivity ad.
ii.

Emotion-evoking

Names a feeling the audience already has. Not "feel this" but "I felt that too." The work works because it locates them.

Headspace meditation as a permission to be unwell. Calm Sleep Stories as a quiet hand on the shoulder.
iii.

Educational

Leaves you smarter. Trust compounds when you've learned something — even a thirty-second reel can carry one good idea.

A Khan Academy explainer. The Brain Health Playbook. Wahed's plain-English glossary.
iv.

Humour

The permission slip. Audiences let humour in when they would not let advertising in. Used sparingly, it amplifies the rest.

Duolingo's mascot speaking like a chaotic friend. Sub-tweets a serious brand wouldn't dare.
v.

Importance

Makes someone feel the stakes. Most marketing is forgettable because nothing important is happening. The good stuff signals: this matters.

Sal Khan introducing Khanmigo on a TED stage. Founders putting their face on serious decisions.
Pick two before you ship. Pick four to be remembered.
