I.Operating principles

Three frameworks I operate by.

Borrowed, with credit, from Matt Gray — and bent to fit the work.

01 — The SELL Formula

Stories. Educate. Lists.

Three structures content has to use if it's going to do work. Stories make ideas stick. Educate earns trust through how-to. Lists structure value to be shared.

The Brain Health Playbook is L. The Muslim Money Guys podcast is S. The MAGNA founder briefings are E.

02 — The Rule of Five

One idea, five surfaces.

One insight, recut natively for five surfaces. Reach multiplies; creative effort doesn't. The same idea travels as a long-form piece, a social carousel, a newsletter, a podcast cut, and a community post.

The Ivory comic characters lived as Instagram carousels, YouTube shorts, LinkedIn long-form, podcast clips, and a 3,000-member WhatsApp thread. One creative system. Five surfaces.

03 — Content is inventory

Profit per piece, not views per piece.

Boring beats viral when it compounds. Every asset is built like inventory you re-use — not output you ship and forget.

The Brain Health Playbook took six weeks. Clinical leads still cite it two years later. Most ad creative doesn't survive a quarter.

II.Teardowns

Four campaigns I read like books.

Brand work that compounded after the launch noise faded.

01

Death of Duo.

Duolingo · Open · Fast Company

A four-year-old social mascot had become its own brand asset. Duolingo staged its death — eulogy, investigation, coffin merch, dead-Duo app icon — then resurrected it through user XP activity two weeks later.

The deposits were the work. The withdrawal was the campaign.

A bit this big is only possible when the voice is already infrastructure. Four years of mascot equity made fourteen days of mythology possible.

Receipts
1.7BImpressions in 14 days
25,560%Mention spike, day one
2×Vs Super Bowl conversation
02

Khanmigo, on a TED stage.

Khan Academy · Open · Sal Khan TED

AI tutoring was about to be commodified by ChatGPT. Sal Khan walked onto the TED stage and framed Khanmigo as a tutor — neither a chatbot nor a productivity feature. Free pilots to school districts followed.

The earned brand was the moat. The launch was the harvest.

When a category is being disrupted, the brand that already exists wins the framing — if it moves at the speed of the disruption.

Receipts
20 yrsBrand equity activated
1TED Talk · one narrative beat
$0Price for school pilots
03

Meditation, as media.

Headspace · Open · Brand system

One of many meditation apps in a category converting on price and feature parity. Headspace took meditation off the app and into media — a Netflix series, partnerships with Snoop Dogg, John Legend, and the NBA, and an illustrated visual system that worked on subway cars, Spotify ads, and Netflix thumbnails alike.

The app became one expression of the brand. The brand was everywhere else.

When the product is one of many, the brand is what makes it singular. Media earns cultural legibility no feature can.

Receipts
70M+Users across 190 countries
$3BHeadspace Health valuation, 2021
2Netflix series · one visual system
04

Sleep Stories, on permanent rotation.

Calm · Open · Sleep Stories

Calm needed an asset class that compounded, not a campaign that peaked. So they recorded Sleep Stories with recognisable voices — McConaughey, Idris Elba, Cillian Murphy, Harry Styles. Each recording functions as both a media moment and a permanent product asset.

Sleep became the audience-builder for the rest of the app.

A product asset built like a campaign earns twice — once on launch, and forever as inventory.
